Plinth is an open-source music player for Android that treats a track as the
music itself, not as a file. It plays the music on your phone and SD card and,
if you want, albums and concerts from the Internet Archive; later versions add
more sources and sync between devices.

<b>What it does now</b>
<ul>
<li>Plays your music in the background, with notification, lock-screen and headset controls, and resumes where you left off.</li>
<li>Reads tags straight from your files on the phone and SD cards: tracks, albums, artists and folders in natural order, and search.</li>
<li>Searches the Internet Archive and plays what it finds: FLAC on Wi-Fi, MP3 on a metered network. Online sources can be turned off.</li>
<li>Likes, listening history and playlists, with M3U, M3U8 and PLS import and M3U8 export.</li>
<li>Keeps your likes, playlists and history through a reinstall: a copy in a folder you choose and in Android backup. Songs you moved, renamed or retagged are found again.</li>
<li>Skips files that can't be played, and explains what to do when your phone's firmware stops background playback.</li>
<li>English and Russian.</li>
</ul>

<b>Privacy</b>
<ul>
<li>No accounts, no ads, no analytics or telemetry.</li>
<li>While online sources are on, searches and streams go to archive.org.</li>
<li>Your likes, playlists and history go to Android backup only when the phone encrypts it; your music and library stay on the device.</li>
<li>A log or a crash report leaves the device only if you share it yourself.</li>
</ul>
